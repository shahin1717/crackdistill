\begin{abstract}\small\setstretch{1.2}
Automated highway pavement defect inspection requires resolving a severe trade-off: capturing \emph{sub-pixel, complex fracture topology} across wide-field pavement surfaces while simultaneously maintaining \emph{ultra-low inference latency ($>100$\,FPS)} on edge hardware. 
We present \textbf{CrackDistill}, an end-to-end framework that transfers the structural and spatial priors of a 224M-parameter foundation vision model (\textbf{\sam}) into an ultra-compact single-stage detector (\textbf{\yolo, 2.84M parameters, 6.2\,MB}) with \textbf{zero runtime teacher overhead}. 

This comprehensive report chronicles the complete development lifecycle from initial scaffolding to final production validation. 
Across eleven distinct experimental campaigns, we address:
(1)~instance conversion of non-convex crack networks via Connected Component Labeling;
(2)~teacher prompting strategies, exposing the failure modes of centroid point prompts on branching cracks and establishing bounding-box support;
(3)~pre-sigmoid logit uncertainty distillation using temperature-scaled Bernoulli KL divergence ($\tau\!=\!3.7769$, $W\!=\!0.9612$);
(4)~the resolution of intermediate feature collapse by replacing raw backbone MSE with multi-scale PANet Neck Channel-Wise Distillation (CWD) and spatial relational pixel affinity;
(5)~the diagnosis of the Out-of-Distribution (OOD) generalization crisis on raw survey photos ($2000\times1500$), tracing the root failure to zero-negative crop bias and sub-pixel downsampling dilution;
(6)~the \textbf{Native Mosaic Breakthrough}, stitching source photos into wide $1920\times720$ composites supervised by native-scale SAM~2 logits;
(7)~a test-time inference engine utilizing \textbf{2D Gaussian apodization tile blending} and multi-scale Gaussian TTA ($640+768$), which boosts megapixel Dice from 0.5061 to \textbf{0.7542} ($+49.0\%$);
and (8)~next-generation loss formulations including \textbf{Resolution-Preserving Mask-KL} achieving all-time record recall (\textbf{0.7945}) and \textbf{Asymmetric Soft Tversky Loss} ($\beta=0.70$) establishing all-time record direct OOD detection (\textbf{0.1459} Mask mAP$_{50}$ / \textbf{0.1820} Box mAP$_{50}$) and precision (\textbf{0.7831}). 
On edge hardware, the distilled student executes at \textbf{107.8\,FPS (9.27\,ms)} on a Tesla~T4 GPU, establishing a production-ready edge deployment paradigm.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Industrial Motivation}
\label{sec:intro}
%=============================================================================

Civil transportation infrastructure represents one of the most capital-intensive public asset classes globally. 
Pavement degradation begins with hairline micro-fractures (1--3\,mm width). If left unaddressed, water ingress, freeze-thaw thermal expansion, and mechanical traffic shear accelerate these micro-fractures into structural spalling and catastrophic potholes. 
Traditional pavement inspection relies heavily on manual visual rating by human surveyors driving at low speeds or inspecting photographic logs. 
This practice is labor-intensive, hazardous, inherently subjective, and physically incapable of scaling to the millions of lane-kilometers surveyed annually.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=boxbg,colframe=boxframe,arc=2mm,title=\textbf{The CrackDistill Industrial Dilemma}]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Sub-Pixel Topological Scale:} In full-frame highway survey cameras ($2560\times1440$ or $2000\times1500$), fine cracks span only 2--6 pixels in width across vast asphalt expanses. Downsampling to standard vision dimensions ($512\times512$) physically dilutes or obliterates up to 80\% of the linear boundary signal.
  \item \textbf{Non-Convex Branching Geometry:} Road cracks exhibit tree-like, highly branching morphologies. Naive point prompting or geometric centroid tracking fails because the centroid of an alligator crack cluster almost always lies on sound, uncracked asphalt.
  \item \textbf{Severe Class Imbalance:} Background asphalt comprises over 99\% of all image pixels, overwhelming gradient updates with negative background noise.
  \item \textbf{Edge Compute Constraints:} Mobile survey vehicles require real-time inference ($>100$\,FPS) on vehicle-mounted embedded hardware with stringent thermal and power bounds ($<30$\,W).
  \item \textbf{Catastrophic Domain Shift (OOD):} Detectors trained exclusively on cropped benchmark tiles experience severe precision collapse when exposed to uncropped survey camera streams.
\end{enumerate}
\end{tcolorbox}
\caption{The core architectural and domain challenges addressed in this project.}
\label{fig:dilemma}
\end{figure}

\noindent\textbf{The Core Hypothesis.}
Foundation vision models such as \sam~\cite{ravi2024sam2} possess unprecedented structural prior representations trained on billions of visual masks, but their 224M parameters and multi-second latency render them completely unsuitable for real-time mobile edge deployment. 
Conversely, real-time edge detectors such as \yolo~\cite{jocher2023ultralytics} achieve $>100$\,FPS on commodity GPUs but struggle to capture thin, continuous boundaries under noisy asphalt texture.
We hypothesize:
\begin{quote}
\emph{By formulating an offline, multi-stage knowledge distillation framework that transfers pre-sigmoid boundary uncertainty, intermediate neck representations, and topological continuity priors from \sam\ into \yolo, the student model can inherit the structural segmentation fidelity of the foundation model while operating with zero runtime teacher dependencies at over 100\,FPS.}
\end{quote}

This report documents the chronological evolution of this project from Day~0 through all eleven major research and engineering phases.

\section{Phase 1: Day 0 Scaffolding, Data Engineering, and Student Selection}
\label{sec:phase1}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Benchmark Dataset Scaffolding}
The experimental foundation is built upon two prominent road crack benchmarks:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Crack500}~\cite{zhang2016crack500}: The primary benchmark containing 1,896 training, 348 validation, and 348 test images. Images are provided as $640\times360$ pixel crops acquired across various asphalt and concrete pavement conditions.
  \item \textbf{DeepCrack}~\cite{liu2019deepcrack}: A secondary benchmark comprising 300 training and 237 test images of concrete surface cracks at higher optical magnification, utilized for cross-dataset transfer evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Connected Component Labeling (CCL) Conversion}
Both benchmarks provide binary semantic masks ($0$ for asphalt background, $255$ for crack pixels). 
Standard instance segmentation frameworks require closed polygon instances for bounding box and prototype mask supervision. 
If an entire binary mask containing multiple interconnected or disjoint crack clusters is converted via naive contour tracing, the converter generates a single colossal bounding box enclosing the entire image. This corrupts per-instance target matching and destroys distillation spatial correspondence.

To solve this, we authored \texttt{convert\_crack500.py} using Connected Component Labeling:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{8-Connected Graph Parsing:} Apply \texttt{cv2.connectedComponentsWithStats} to partition disjoint crack filaments into discrete instance objects.
  \item \textbf{Noise Thresholding:} Filter out disconnected speckle artifacts containing fewer than 50 pixels ($\approx 0.02\%$ of image area).
  \item \textbf{Polygon Vectorization:} Extract discrete instance boundaries and normalize coordinates into YOLO polygon format:
  \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{P}_k = \left\{ (x_i, y_i) \mid x_i = \frac{X_i}{W}, y_i = \frac{Y_i}{H} \right\}_{i=1}^{N_k}
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Student Architecture: YOLOv11n-seg vs. YOLOv8n-seg}
While preliminary studies utilized \yolov\ (3.26M parameters, 12.0 GFLOPs), our primary student architecture was updated to \textbf{\yolo} (2.84M parameters, 6.2\,MB FP32 checkpoint size, 10.4 GFLOPs). 
\yolo\ introduces the \textbf{C3k2} and \textbf{C2PSA} (Cross-Stage Partial with Spatial Attention) modules, providing higher receptive-field expressiveness per parameter. 
The mask generation head uses a prototype segmentation design: the backbone and PANet neck output $k=32$ prototype mask channels at $160\times160$ resolution, which are linearly combined with instance-specific mask coefficients predicted by the detection heads.

\section{Phase 2: Teacher Prompting, Offline Caching, and Distillation Mathematics}
\label{sec:phase2}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{The Centroid Prompting Trap}
\sam\ is a promptable segmentation model requiring visual prompts (points or bounding boxes). 
In early iterations, we evaluated prompting SAM~2 with the geometric centroid of each ground-truth instance:
\begin{equation}
  (\bar{x}_k, \bar{y}_k) = \left( \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_k|} \sum_{(x,y) \in \mathcal{M}_k} x, \; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{M}_k|} \sum_{(x,y) \in \mathcal{M}_k} y \right)
\end{equation}
\textbf{The Failure Mode:} Road cracks exhibit concave, highly branching geometries (such as longitudinal fractures with lateral arms or closed alligator meshes). 
The geometric centroid of a U-shaped or multi-branch crack almost invariably falls on sound asphalt \emph{between} the crack filaments. 
Prompting SAM~2 at this coordinate caused the foundation model to segment the background asphalt or predict an empty mask (false-negative teacher logit).

\textbf{The Solution:} We transitioned to instance bounding-box prompts:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{B}_k = [x_{\min} - \delta, \; y_{\min} - \delta, \; x_{\max} + \delta, \; y_{\max} + \delta]
\end{equation}
with an expansion margin $\delta=4$\,px. 
Bounding-box prompts provide SAM~2 with the complete spatial domain of the fracture network, yielding consistent, highly accurate boundary segmentations.

\subsection{Pre-Sigmoid Logits and Dark Knowledge Extraction}
Rather than distilling thresholded binary masks, we implemented \texttt{generate\_teacher\_logits.py} to intercept the pre-sigmoid mask decoder logits $\mathbf{Z}^t \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 256 \times 256}$ of SAM~2 Large. 
In binary masks, all edge pixels are quantized to 0 or 1, discarding the model's confidence landscape. 
In contrast, SAM~2 pre-sigmoid logits represent calibrated spatial uncertainty:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Deep crack interior: $z^t \gg +3.0 \implies \sigma(z^t) \approx 1.0$
  \item Clear background asphalt: $z^t \ll -3.0 \implies \sigma(z^t) \approx 0.0$
  \item Crack boundary transition: $z^t \approx 0.0 \implies \sigma(z^t) \approx 0.5$
\end{itemize}
These transition bands encode the ``dark knowledge'' of sub-pixel fracture edges. 
All teacher logits across Crack500 and DeepCrack were generated offline on a GPU cluster and cached to disk as FP16 tensors, eliminating runtime teacher memory requirements during student training.

\subsection{Temperature-Scaled Bernoulli KL Formulation}
Binary segmentation at each pixel $(u, v)$ can be viewed as a Bernoulli distribution. 
For student logit $z^s$ and teacher logit $z^t$, we introduce temperature parameter $\tau > 0$ to soften the probability distributions:
\begin{equation}
  p^t(u, v) = \sigma\left(\frac{z^t(u, v)}{\tau}\right), \qquad p^s(u, v) = \sigma\left(\frac{z^s(u, v)}{\tau}\right)
\end{equation}
The temperature-scaled Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence loss is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{Mask-KL}} = \tau^2 \cdot \frac{1}{HW} \sum_{u=1}^H \sum_{v=1}^W \left[ p^t \log \frac{p^t}{p^s} + (1 - p^t) \log \frac{1 - p^t}{1 - p^s} \right]
\end{equation}
Scaling by $\tau^2$ ensures that the gradient magnitude $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{Mask-KL}}}{\partial z^s}$ remains invariant to changes in temperature:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}_{\text{Mask-KL}}}{\partial z^s} = \tau \cdot \left( \sigma\left(\frac{z^s}{\tau}\right) - \sigma\left(\frac{z^t}{\tau}\right) \right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Optuna Automated Hyperparameter Search}
We conducted an automated Optuna study across 40 trials to optimize temperature $\tau$ and KD loss weight $W_{\text{KD}}$. 
The search space evaluated $\tau \in [1.0, 8.0]$ and $W_{\text{KD}} \in [0.1, 5.0]$.
The study converged to an optimal Pareto frontier:
\begin{equation}
  \tau^* = 3.7769, \qquad W_{\text{KD}}^* = 0.9612
\end{equation}
This confirmed that standard classification temperatures ($\tau=1.0$ or $\tau=2.0$) were insufficient to capture the wide dynamic range of pre-sigmoid transformer logits, whereas $\tau \approx 3.78$ evenly smoothed boundary gradients without washing out structural separation.

\section{Phase 3: Intermediate Feature Distillation (LayerKD) \& Neck Hooking}
\label{sec:phase3}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{The Failure of Naive Backbone MSE}
Encouraged by output mask improvements, we initially investigated intermediate feature distillation directly from the SAM~2 backbone to the \yolo\ backbone. 
We hooked intermediate feature stages and applied Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} = \frac{1}{CHW} \sum_{c,h,w} \left( \mathcal{F}^t_{c,h,w} - \text{Proj}(\mathcal{F}^s)_{c,h,w} \right)^2
\end{equation}
\textbf{The Empirical Failure:} This configuration resulted in a \textbf{$-2.14\%$ drop in Mask mAP$_{50}$}. 
\textbf{Root Cause Analysis:} SAM~2 Large utilizes a 224M-parameter Hiera Vision Transformer backbone with windowed global self-attention. 
\yolo\ utilizes a 2.84M-parameter convolutional backbone. 
Forcing the tiny CNN backbone to mimic the channel-wise activation magnitudes of a $79\times$ larger Vision Transformer over-constrained the student's limited parameter budget, destroying its ability to extract low-level edge features.

\subsection{PANet Neck Channel-Wise Distillation (CWD)}
To bypass the backbone capacity gap, we shifted distillation from the backbone to the \textbf{Path Aggregation Network (PANet) neck} (layers 12, 15, and 18 of \yolo), where multi-scale features are already integrated. 
Furthermore, we replaced scale-sensitive MSE with \textbf{Channel-Wise Distillation (CWD)}~\cite{shu2021cwd}.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=boxbg,colframe=boxframe,arc=2mm,title=\textbf{Mathematical Formulation: Channel-Wise Distillation (CWD)}]
Instead of normalizing across channels at each spatial pixel, CWD applies a 2D spatial softmax \emph{within each channel}:
\begin{equation}
  \phi(F)_{c,i,j} = \frac{\exp\left( F_{c,i,j} / \tau \right)}{\sum_{i'=1}^H \sum_{j'=1}^W \exp\left( F_{c,i',j'} / \tau \right)}
\end{equation}
This transforms each channel feature map into a spatial probability distribution representing where the model focuses attention for that specific feature detector.
The distillation loss is the sum of KL divergences across all $C$ channels:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{CWD}} = \tau^2 \cdot \frac{1}{C} \sum_{c=1}^C \sum_{i=1}^H \sum_{j=1}^W \phi(F^t)_{c,i,j} \cdot \log\left( \frac{\phi(F^t)_{c,i,j}}{\phi(\hat{F}^s)_{c,i,j}} \right)
\end{equation}
where $\hat{F}^s = \text{CAP}(F^s)$ is the student feature map projected via a $1\times1$ Cross-Architecture Projector (CAP) with LayerNorm.
\end{tcolorbox}
\caption{Formulation of Channel-Wise Distillation applied to \yolo\ PANet neck.}
\label{fig:cwd}
\end{figure}

By normalizing spatially within channels, CWD eliminates differences in absolute feature activation scales between ViT and CNN architectures, distilling only the relative spatial attention patterns.

\subsection{Spatial Relational Pixel Affinity KD}
In crack detection, whether pixel $A$ is connected to pixel $B$ (topological continuity) is more critical than absolute intensity. 
In variant \texttt{04\_affinity}, we introduced a non-local spatial pixel affinity matrix:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A}(u, v) = \frac{f_u \cdot f_v}{\|f_u\|_2 \|f_v\|_2}
\end{equation}
Supervising student affinity matrices with teacher affinities forces the student to learn that separate crack filaments belong to the same topological network, achieving our highest in-domain segmentation accuracy (\textbf{0.5569 Mask mAP$_{50}$}).

\section{Phase 5: The Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization Crisis}
\label{sec:phase5}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Catastrophic Generalization Collapse}
While in-domain metrics reached peak accuracy on $640\times360$ test crops, evaluating these models on \emph{raw, uncropped road survey imagery} ($2000\times1500$ and $2560\times1440$) revealed an alarming generalization drop:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item In-Domain Mask mAP$_{50}$: $\approx 0.5400 - 0.5569$
  \item Direct Full-Resolution OOD Mask mAP$_{50}$: $\mathbf{0.0848}$ ($\approx 84\%$ performance collapse!)
  \item Direct Full-Resolution Megapixel Dice: $\mathbf{0.1651 - 0.2414}$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Forensic Investigation: The Three Root Causes}
We conducted an empirical audit across the raw data files, uncovering three underlying mechanisms:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{The Zero-Negative Pavement Bias:} 
  Inspection of \texttt{crack500\_yolo/labels/train/} revealed that \textbf{all 1,896 training files contained crack instances (0 background-only tiles)}. 
  The original Crack500 dataset creators cropped full photos into a $4\times4$ grid and discarded all empty background tiles before publishing. 
  Consequently, the student model had never once been trained to output an empty mask on plain asphalt. When presented with full survey photos (which are $>95\%$ clean asphalt), the model hallucinated crack boundaries on road texture, aggregate, and shadows.
  \item \textbf{Sub-Pixel Interpolation Dilution:} 
  A hairline crack measuring 4 pixels in width on a $2000\times1500$ photo shrinks to:
  \begin{equation}
    w_{\text{downsampled}} = 4 \times \frac{512}{2000} \approx 1.02\text{ pixels}
  \end{equation}
  Bilinear downsampling blurs this single-pixel fracture into neighboring asphalt pixels, severely reducing contrast below the detection threshold.
  \item \textbf{Native Crop Dimensions:} 
  We verified that the true native Crack500 files on disk were $640\times360$, not $512\times512$. Training at \texttt{imgsz=512} was needlessly downsampling source pixels prior to training.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Phase 6: The Native Mosaic Breakthrough}
\label{sec:phase6}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Composite Mosaic Stitching Engine}
Because Crack500 crop filenames contain spatial offset metadata (\eg, \texttt{\{stem\}\_\{x\}\_\{y\}.jpg} on a $640\times360$ stride), we authored \texttt{mine\_negative\_and\_mosaic\_tiles.py} to reconstruct contiguous source regions:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Reconstructed \textbf{250 full-scale composite training images} with resolutions up to \textbf{$1920\times720$ pixels}.
  \item Merged ground-truth polygons using \texttt{build\_augmented\_training\_set.py} to produce a unified scale-diverse dataset (\texttt{crack500\_yolo\_augmented}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Native-Scale SAM 2 Logit Generation}
Crucially, foundation model distillation had previously been restricted to the same $640\times360$ crops. 
In \texttt{OODimprovements/02}, we passed the reconstructed $1920\times720$ mosaic composites through \sam, generating true wide-field teacher logits:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{Z}^t_{\text{mosaic}} \in \mathbb{R}^{M \times 720 \times 1920}
\end{equation}
This enabled the teacher to distill structural context across long, multi-branch crack systems spanning multiple original tiles.

\subsection{Empirical Breakthrough: \texttt{03\_mosaic\_native}}
In \texttt{OODimprovements/03}, we trained \yolo\ on the augmented mosaic dataset at native \texttt{imgsz=640} using merged native teacher logits.
The results established a major empirical breakthrough:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Direct OOD Mask mAP$_{50}$:} Jumped from $0.0848 \to \mathbf{0.1409}$ (\textbf{$+66.2\%$ relative improvement}).
  \item \textbf{Direct OOD Box mAP$_{50}$:} Jumped from $0.0848 \to \mathbf{0.1747}$ (\textbf{$+106.0\%$ relative improvement --- more than doubled!}).
  \item \textbf{Direct OOD Mask mAP$_{50-95}$:} Jumped from $0.0196 \to \mathbf{0.0373}$ (\textbf{$+90.3\%$ gain}).
\end{itemize}

%=============================================================================
\section{Phase 7: Test-Time Slicing \& 2D Gaussian TTA Engine}
\label{sec:phase7}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{The Sliced Sliding-Window Paradigm}
While mosaic training improved direct downsampled metrics, physical downsampling still degrades sub-pixel cracks. 
To fully resolve this at test time, we authored a sliced sliding-window inference engine (\texttt{OODimprovements/04} and \texttt{08}). 
Full-resolution photos ($2000\times1500$) are partitioned into sliding patches of size $W_p \times H_p = 640\times640$ with a stride overlap of $S_{\text{overlap}} = 25\%$.

\subsection{2D Gaussian Apodization Blending}
Standard sliding-window implementations use uniform averaging or hard non-maximum suppression (NMS) in overlapping regions. 
This introduces severe edge seam artifacts: convolutional network predictions near patch boundaries suffer from boundary zero-padding effects, creating false positive edge steps.

To eliminate boundary seams, we implemented \textbf{2D Gaussian Apodization Blending} (inspired by nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnu}):
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{G}(x, y) = \exp\left( -\frac{1}{2} \left[ \left( \frac{x - \mu_x}{\sigma_x} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{y - \mu_y}{\sigma_y} \right)^2 \right] \right)
\end{equation}
where $\mu_x = \mu_y = \frac{640}{2} = 320$ and $\sigma_x = \sigma_y = \frac{640}{8} = 80$.
The global reconstructed prediction map $\mathcal{M}_{\text{global}}$ is the normalized weighted accumulation across all patches $k$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{M}_{\text{global}}(X, Y) = \frac{\sum_k \mathcal{G}(x_k, y_k) \cdot \mathcal{P}_k(x_k, y_k)}{\sum_k \mathcal{G}(x_k, y_k)}
\end{equation}
Pixels near patch centers receive maximum weight ($1.0$), while boundary pixels near edges taper smoothly to zero, completely extinguishing tile-boundary seam discontinuities.

\subsection{Multi-Scale Gaussian Test-Time Augmentation (TTA)}
In \texttt{OODimprovements/08}, we extended the engine to \textbf{Multi-Scale Gaussian TTA}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Scale 1: $640\times640$ patches (25\% overlap, Gaussian apodization)
  \item Scale 2: $768\times768$ patches (25\% overlap, Gaussian apodization)
\end{itemize}
Fused prediction maps capture macro-structural fractures and micro hairline cracks simultaneously.
\textbf{The Breakthrough Result:} On raw uncropped megapixel photos, the multi-scale Gaussian TTA engine boosted Dice from $0.5061 \to \mathbf{0.7542}$ (\textbf{$+49.0\%$ relative improvement}), achieving a recall of \textbf{0.7848} with zero model retraining.

\section{Phase 8: Advanced Next-Gen Distillation Formulations}
\label{sec:phase8}
%=============================================================================

Following the mosaic and test-time breakthroughs, we developed four advanced distillation formulations (\texttt{OODimprovements/05--08}):

\subsection{Notebook 05: Two-Stage Transfer Fine-Tuning}
Rather than training on mosaics from scratch for 150 epochs (5.5 hours), \texttt{05\_run\_twostage\_mosaic\_native\_tune.ipynb} evaluated transfer fine-tuning:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Stage 1:} In-domain crop pre-training (\texttt{04\_affinity} checkpoint).
  \item \textbf{Stage 2:} 50 epochs of mosaic fine-tuning at $10\times$ reduced learning rate ($\text{lr}_0 = 0.001$).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Result:} Achieved \textbf{0.1130 direct OOD Mask mAP$_{50}$} and \textbf{0.7468 Multi-Scale TTA Dice} in only 1.1 hours, delivering $70\%$ compute reduction while retaining $>98\%$ of the SOTA performance.

\subsection{Notebook 06: Resolution-Preserving Mask-KL}
In standard \yolo, the prototype mask head produces prototypes at stride 4 ($160\times160$ for a $640\times640$ input). 
Downsampling teacher logits to $160\times160$ before KL divergence causes sub-pixel prototype dilution. 
In \texttt{06\_run\_resolution\_preserving\_kd.ipynb}, we inverted this flow:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathcal{M}}^s = \text{BilinearUpsample}\left( \mathcal{M}^s, \; \text{size}=(H_{\text{teacher}}, W_{\text{teacher}}) \right)
\end{equation}
Student prototype masks are upsampled to the native teacher resolution ($640\times360$) \emph{before} computing KL divergence.
\textbf{Result:} Achieved the \textbf{all-time highest high-IoU metric (0.2050 Mask mAP$_{50-95}$)} and \textbf{0.761 Box precision}. When evaluated in the TTA engine, it established the \textbf{all-time highest Recall record: 0.7945 👑}.

\subsection{Notebook 07: Asymmetric Soft Tversky Loss}
To prevent crack branch fragmentation, \texttt{07\_run\_asymmetric\_tversky\_kd.ipynb} introduced the Asymmetric Soft Tversky loss:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{Tversky}} = 1 - \frac{\sum_i p_i y_i + \epsilon}{\sum_i p_i y_i + \alpha \sum_i p_i (1 - y_i) + \beta \sum_i (1 - p_i) y_i + \epsilon}
\end{equation}
By setting $\beta = 0.70$ and $\alpha = 0.30$, false negatives (missed crack pixels) are penalized $2.33\times$ more severely than false positives. 
Combined with PANet Neck CWD on layers 12, 15, and 18, this loss forces the student to maintain unbroken crack network continuity.
\textbf{Result:} Notebook 07 established the \textbf{all-time SOTA direct OOD record}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Direct OOD Mask mAP$_{50}$:} $\mathbf{0.1459}$ 👑 ($+72.1\%$ over baseline, $+3.5\%$ over \texttt{03\_mosaic})
  \item \textbf{Direct OOD Box mAP$_{50}$:} $\mathbf{0.1820}$ 👑 ($+114.6\%$ over baseline, $+4.2\%$ over \texttt{03\_mosaic})
  \item \textbf{Direct OOD Mask mAP$_{50-95}$:} $\mathbf{0.0394}$ 👑
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Notebook 08: Diagnostic and Checkpoint Discovery Resolution}
During initial evaluation in Notebook 08, \texttt{07\_asymmetric\_tversky} reported an anomaly ($0.0000$ Dice). 
Our diagnostic audit identified a dictionary deduplication collision:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Every fine-tuned checkpoint under \texttt{.../weights/best.pt} was assigned the tag \texttt{"best"}. 
  \item Because \texttt{06} had already claimed key \texttt{"best"}, \texttt{07}'s fine-tuned weights were skipped. 
  \item Instead, the scanner evaluated \texttt{yolo11n-seg.pt} (the untrained base COCO model downloaded during initialization), which predicted 0 cracks.
\end{itemize}
We updated \texttt{build\_ood\_improvement\_notebooks.py} to tag checkpoints with their experiment directory names and filter base stock weights. 
Re-running the patched Notebook 08 validated the true performance of \texttt{07}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Multi-Scale TTA Precision:} $\mathbf{0.7831}$ 👑 (\textbf{All-Time Project Record!})
  \item \textbf{Direct Resize Dice on Full Photos:} $\mathbf{0.5141}$ 👑 (\textbf{All-Time Project Record!})
  \item \textbf{Multi-Scale TTA Dice:} $\mathbf{0.7427}$ (Precision: 0.7831, Recall: 0.7546)
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work and Research Roadmap}
\label{sec:future}
%=============================================================================

While the CrackDistill pipeline has achieved its primary objectives, our findings point to several promising research extensions:

\subsection{1. The Unified "Super-Recipe"}
Our empirical results prove that different loss formulations optimize complementary axes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \texttt{06\_res\_preserving} maximizes \textbf{Recall} (0.7945).
  \item \texttt{07\_asymmetric\_tversky} maximizes \textbf{Precision} (0.7831).
  \item \texttt{06\_layerkd} aligns \textbf{Internal Neck Channels}.
  \item \texttt{04\_affinity} preserves \textbf{Spatial Topology}.
\end{itemize}
We propose training a unified student integrating all four mechanisms into a single composite objective:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{Super}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{YOLO}} + \lambda_1 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Tversky}}^{(\beta=0.7)} + \lambda_2 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Res-KL}}^{(\text{upsampled})} + \lambda_3 \mathcal{L}_{\text{CWD}}^{(\text{neck})} + \lambda_4 \mathcal{L}_{\text{Affinity}}
\end{equation}
This offers a direct path toward surpassing 0.8000 megapixel Dice.

\subsection{2. Real-World Negative Pavement Tile Injection}
As revealed in Section~\ref{sec:phase5}, Crack500 contains zero background-only training tiles. 
By collecting a modest auxiliary dataset of 300--500 verified crack-free highway pavement images (featuring oil stains, joint sealant lines, shadow cast, and varied aggregate sizes), the model can be explicitly supervised with empty target masks ($y_i=0$). 
This will eliminate residual false-positive alarms on coarse aggregate surfaces.

\subsection{3. INT8 TensorRT Compilation for Embedded SoCs}
While 107.8\,FPS is achieved in FP32 on Tesla T4, compiling the student via TensorRT with Post-Training Quantization (INT8) targeting the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano (15\,W) or Xavier NX will enable direct deployment in vehicle-mounted smart camera enclosures with sub-5\,ms latency.

\subsection{4. Multi-Class Distress Taxonomy Extension}
The current framework treats crack detection as binary instance segmentation. 
Future work will extend the label taxonomy to categorize specific distress types:
(1)~transverse thermal cracks, (2)~longitudinal wheel-path cracks, (3)~alligator fatigue clusters, and (4)~surface potholes. 
Since SAM~2 provides class-agnostic structural segmentations, the multi-task head of YOLOv11 can be fine-tuned for distress classification without modifying the core distillation engine.

\subsection{5. Cross-Domain Zero-Shot Evaluation}
Evaluating the trained model on international road datasets across different continents (such as EdmCrack600, GAPs384, and CFD) will quantify domain transferability across diverse asphalt mixes and climate wear profiles.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%=============================================================================

This report presented \textbf{CrackDistill}, a comprehensive framework transferring structural segmentation capability from \sam\ (224M parameters) into \yolo\ (2.84M parameters) for automated road crack inspection. 
By resolving instance conversion with Connected Components Labeling, extracting pre-sigmoid boundary logits, replacing raw backbone MSE with PANet Neck CWD, diagnosing the zero-negative OOD crisis, reconstructing native mosaic composites, deploying a 2D Gaussian apodization TTA engine, and introducing Resolution-Preserving Mask-KL and Asymmetric Soft Tversky losses, the project achieved:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{0.5569 Mask mAP$_{50}$} in-domain segmentation peak (\texttt{04\_affinity});
  \item \textbf{0.1459 Mask mAP$_{50}$ / 0.1820 Box mAP$_{50}$} direct OOD detection SOTA (\texttt{07\_tversky});
  \item \textbf{0.7831 TTA Precision} all-time record (\texttt{07\_tversky});
  \item \textbf{0.7945 TTA Recall} all-time record (\texttt{06\_res\_preserving});
  \item \textbf{0.7542 Megapixel Tiled Dice} breakthrough (\texttt{03\_mosaic});
  \item \textbf{107.8 FPS (9.27\,ms)} inference throughput on Tesla T4 edge hardware.
\end{itemize}
These results confirm that foundation segmentation models can serve as highly effective structural teachers for edge-deployable inspection networks, bridging the gap between foundation model intelligence and real-time civil infrastructure maintenance.
