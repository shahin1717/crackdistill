{\LARGE\bfseries CrackDistill: Transferring Structural Priors from a\\[0.3em]
Foundation Segmentation Model to a Real-Time Edge Detector\\[0.3em]
for Road Crack Inspection}

\vspace{1.5cm}
{\large Shahin}\\[0.4em]
{\normalsize August 2026}\\[0.4em]
{\small \texttt{/home/shahin/distill} --- CrackDistill Project Repository}

\vspace{1cm}\rule{11cm}{0.4pt}\vspace{1cm}

\begin{abstract}\setstretch{1.2}
Automated road crack detection must simultaneously satisfy two contradictory
constraints: \emph{rich structural understanding} of sub-pixel, branching crack
topology, and \emph{real-time inference speed} for large-scale survey deployment
on commodity hardware.
We present \textbf{CrackDistill}, a knowledge-distillation (KD) framework that
transfers the spatial priors of \textbf{SAM~2 Large (224M parameters)} into a
single-stage nano student (\textbf{\yolo, 2.84M parameters, 6.2\,MB}) with
\emph{zero runtime teacher dependency}.
The student is trained on the Crack500 benchmark (1,896 images) using
pre-computed, pre-sigmoid soft logits from SAM~2 as distillation targets,
supervised by a temperature-scaled Bernoulli KL-divergence loss
($\tau\!=\!3.7769$).
Our comprehensive ablation covers six loss configurations, two datasets
(Crack500, DeepCrack), three distillation regimes (output-only, intermediate
neck, combined), native-scale mosaic augmentation, and a tiled sliding-window
evaluation engine.
The best in-domain variant (\texttt{04\_affinity}) achieves
\textbf{Mask mAP$_{50}$\,=\,0.5569} ($+3.1\%$ over baseline), while the best OOD
variant (\texttt{03\_dilated}) achieves \textbf{OOD Mask mAP$_{50}$\,=\,0.1007}
($+18.7\%$) on uncropped $2000\times1500$ road photos.
Tiled sliding-window inference with 2D Gaussian apodization recovers
\textbf{52--83\,\%} of the resolution-induced Dice loss with no retraining.
The student runs at \textbf{107.8\,FPS (9.27\,ms)} on a Tesla~T4.
Cross-dataset transfer experiments demonstrate up to $+19.0\%$ Mask~mAP$_{50}$
gain, confirming that SAM~2 spatial priors act as a domain-agnostic structural
regulariser.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Motivation}
\label{sec:intro}
%=============================================================================

Road infrastructure maintenance is one of the largest recurring expenditures
for municipal and national governments.
Pavement crack detection---the early identification of surface fractures before
they propagate into structural failures---is currently performed by human
inspectors, an approach that is costly, inconsistent, and unscalable to the
hundreds of thousands of lane-kilometres surveyed annually.

Deep learning has transformed visual inspection, yet road crack segmentation
exposes several fundamental challenges:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \textbf{Sub-pixel topology.} Hairline cracks are 4--8 pixels wide in
    raw $2000\times1500$ survey photos. Downsampling to $512\times512$ physically
    erases $\sim$80\% of the crack width by interpolation.
  \item \textbf{Non-convex, branching morphology.} The geometric centroid of a
    branching crack network typically lies on bare asphalt between branches,
    causing naive point-prompting to produce false-negative teacher responses.
  \item \textbf{Background homogeneity.} 99\% of pavement pixels are background
    asphalt, creating severe class imbalance that drowns gradient signals from
    thin boundaries.
  \item \textbf{Edge deployment.} Real-time survey vehicles require $>100$\,FPS
    on compact GPU hardware. Foundation models with 224M parameters are
    undeployable.
  \item \textbf{Domain shift.} Models trained on cropped crops collapse on
    uncropped megapixel imagery due to resolution and appearance mismatch.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Foundation models as structural teachers.}
SAM~2~\cite{ravi2024sam2} is a 224M-parameter vision transformer producing
spatially calibrated soft probability maps that encode fine boundary
uncertainty---the ``dark knowledge''~\cite{hinton2015distilling} that small
detectors lack.

\noindent\textbf{The CrackDistill hypothesis.}
\begin{quote}
\emph{A nano YOLOv11n-seg detector, trained on the pre-sigmoid output logits
of SAM~2 Large with temperature-scaled KL divergence, will learn superior crack
boundary representations compared to a student trained on binary labels alone,
while retaining full real-time inference speed.}
\end{quote}

This paper presents the complete experimental development of this hypothesis,
from initial scaffold and dataset conversion through progressive loss design,
hyperparameter optimisation, ablation, the final production variant suite,
intermediate layer-wise distillation, OOD evaluation, native-scale mosaic
augmentation, and future directions including ensemble logit fusion.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Road Crack Detection}

Traditional methods relied on hand-crafted filters (Canny, Otsu thresholding).
Encoder-decoder architectures~\cite{ronneberger2015unet,chen2017deeplab}
improved mean IoU substantially but struggled with thin cracks.
YOLO-family detectors achieve real-time detection but were not designed for
thin linear structures~\cite{jocher2023ultralytics}.
Recent high-resolution training ($1024\times1024$, 89.6\% mIoU~\cite{crack_hires_2025})
shows that native pixel detail is critical for cross-scale generalisation,
motivating our mosaic augmentation pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:mosaic}).

\subsection{Knowledge Distillation}

Knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling} trains a compact student to
mimic a large teacher's soft predictions.
The temperature-scaled KL formulation
$\mathcal{L}_\text{KD} = \tau^2\,\mathrm{KL}(\sigma(z_t/\tau)\|\sigma(z_s/\tau))$
transfers boundary uncertainty that binary labels discard.

Intermediate feature distillation~\cite{romero2015fitnets,zagoruyko2017at}
extends KD to internal representations.
Channel-Wise Distillation (CWD)~\cite{shu2021cwd} applies per-channel
spatial-softmax KL, avoiding scale-invariance failures of raw MSE across
architecturally dissimilar networks.
Masked Generative Distillation (MGD)~\cite{yang2022mgd} reconstructs teacher
features from partially masked student features, acting as a regulariser.

\subsection{SAM~2 as a Distillation Teacher}

SAM~2~\cite{ravi2024sam2} produces FPN-style multi-scale features at strides
4, 8, 16, 32, and dense mask decoder logits at $256\times256$.
We show (Section~\ref{sec:prompttrap}) that tight bounding-box prompts strictly
outperform centroid point prompts for non-convex crack morphologies.

\section{Datasets and Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:data}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Crack500}

Crack500~\cite{zhang2016crack500}: \textbf{1,896 train / 348 val / 348 test}
images at $640\times360$ pixels, cropped from full-resolution pavement photos.
\textbf{Key challenge:} All 1,896 training images contain crack pixels (zero
empty negatives), biasing the model toward over-predicting on background
textures (gravel, oil stains)---discovered empirically during OOD evaluation
(Section~\ref{sec:mosaic}).

\subsection{DeepCrack}

DeepCrack~\cite{liu2019deepcrack}: 300 training images of concrete-surface
cracks at higher magnification.
Used as a secondary evaluation domain for cross-dataset zero-shot transfer
(Section~\ref{sec:crossdataset}).

\subsection{Instance Conversion via Connected Components}

Both datasets provide binary semantic masks.
Conversion to YOLO polygon format via \texttt{convert\_crack500.py}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item OpenCV \texttt{cv2.connectedComponents} separates touching crack
    segments.
  \item Components $<50$ pixels filtered (noise removal).
  \item Each component boundary converted to normalised YOLO polygon
    coordinates.
\end{enumerate}
Without connected-component separation, touching cracks merge into one giant
polygon, inflating bounding boxes and corrupting per-instance KD target
assignment.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

SAM~2 processes each image with a box prompt and produces pre-sigmoid logits
$\mathbf{Z}^t_i \in \mathbb{R}^{256\times256}$, stored offline before training.
The student \yolo\ outputs instance mask logits
$\mathbf{Z}^s_j\in\mathbb{R}^{256\times256}$.
The training objective is:
\[
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_\text{task} + \mathcal{L}_\text{KD}
\]
where $\mathcal{L}_\text{task}$ is the standard YOLO detection + segmentation
loss.

\section{Phase 1: Architecture Scaffold and Baseline}
\label{sec:phase1}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Modular Pipeline Design}

Development began with a \texttt{CrackDistillTrainer} class and a unified
\texttt{config.yaml} controlling all hyperparameters.
A task registry maps configurations to dataset loaders, loss heads, and student
adapters, enabling rapid switching between loss configurations without touching
training loop code---essential for the later ablation study.

\subsection{Student Model Selection}

\textbf{YOLOv11n-seg} (Ultralytics, 2024) was selected as primary student:
2.84M parameters, 6.2\,MB, 10.2\,GFLOPs, C3k2 and C2PSA attention blocks,
native instance segmentation with Task-Aligned Learning (TAL) assigner.
\textbf{YOLOv8n-seg} (3.26M parameters, 12\,GFLOPs) is retained as an
architecture-matched secondary baseline.

\subsection{Gold Baseline}

\yolo\ fine-tuned without any distillation loss, 150 epochs, Crack500:
\[
\text{Mask mAP}_{50} = \mathbf{0.5400},\quad
\text{Box mAP}_{50} = \mathbf{0.5970}
\quad [\text{EXP-10}]
\]

\section{Phase 2: Offline Teacher Logit Generation}
\label{sec:phase2}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Why Soft Logits, Not Binary Masks}

At crack edges, SAM~2's logit $z_t \approx 0$, so $\sigma(z_t)\approx 0.5$,
encoding spatial uncertainty about the precise boundary.
Storing \textbf{raw pre-sigmoid logits} preserves this ``dark
knowledge''~\cite{hinton2015distilling}.
Disk footprint: 1,896 files $\times$ $256\times256$ float32 $\approx$ 500\,MB.

\subsection{SAM~2 Prompting Strategy: The Prompt Trap}
\label{sec:prompttrap}

We compared two prompting strategies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Box prompt:} tight axis-aligned bounding box of the binary mask.
  \item \textbf{Centroid point prompt:} 2D centroid of the mask polygon.
\end{itemize}
EXP-14 (Box) = EXP-15 (Box+Centroid) = $0.5468$ Mask mAP$_{50}$.
Standalone centroid-only: $0.5254$ Mask mAP$_{50}$.
Root cause: for a non-convex, winding crack, the centroid lies on bare asphalt
between branches, causing SAM~2 to attend to background.
\textbf{Box prompts are used exclusively.}

\subsection{Pre-Computation Pipeline}

Script \texttt{scripts/generate\_teacher\_logits.py} runs SAM~2 Large in
evaluation mode (no gradients) and saves the mask head's pre-sigmoid output per
image.
A mandatory pre-training assertion $N_\text{logits}>0$ prevents silent
training with zero teacher supervision---a safety check motivated by
Section~\ref{sec:bugs}.

%=============================================================================
\section{Phase 3: First-Generation Distillation Loss}
\label{sec:phase3}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Global Boundary Loss}

First distillation attempt: global canvas-level BCE between student and
teacher soft targets, weighted toward high teacher uncertainty:
\[
W_\text{bnd} = 1 - 2|\sigma(z_t)-0.5|,\quad
\mathcal{L}_\text{bnd} = \mathrm{BCE}(\sigma(z_s),\sigma(z_t))\odot W_\text{bnd}
\]
Early gain: $+2.1$ Mask mAP$_{50}$ points [EXP-01, EXP-02].
Limitation: global comparison mismatches student predictions for instance~$A$
against teacher logits for instance~$B$ in multi-crack images.

\subsection{Multi-Scale Feature MSE (Attempt)}
\label{sec:feat_mse}

Intermediate feature MSE at backbone layers $[2,5,8]$ with trainable
$1\times1$ projections:
\[
\mathcal{L}_\text{feat} = \sum_{i\in\{2,5,8\}}\mathrm{MSE}(P_i(S_i),\;\mathrm{Resize}(T_i))
\]
\textbf{Result: performance degraded.}
Cause: the \emph{79$\times$ capacity gap}---SAM~2's ViT (224M) produces
globally context-aware features; YOLOv11's CNN (2.84M) produces locally biased
features. Raw L2 MSE between these incompatible representations causes gradient
dilution, amplified by 99\% background asphalt.
This motivated Channel-Wise Distillation (Section~\ref{sec:layerkd}).

%=============================================================================
\section{Phase 4: Per-Instance Target Matching}
\label{sec:phase4}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Task-Aligned Assigner Integration}

We patched the YOLO training step to intercept the \texttt{TaskAlignedAssigner}
(TAL), providing:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{fg\_mask}: foreground positive anchors.
  \item \texttt{target\_gt\_idx}: anchor-to-ground-truth instance mapping.
\end{itemize}
Each student anchor is now aligned to \emph{exactly} the SAM~2 logit of its
corresponding ground-truth instance.

\subsection{Per-Instance Soft Mask KL Divergence}
\label{sec:mask_kd}

\begin{align}
q_j &= \sigma\!\left(\frac{z^t_j}{\tau}\right),\quad
p_j = \sigma\!\left(\frac{z^s_j}{\tau}\right) \\
\mathcal{L}_\text{mask-KD} &= \tau^2\cdot\frac{1}{N_\text{pos}}
  \sum_{j=1}^{N_\text{pos}}\mathrm{KL}(q_j\|p_j)
\label{eq:mask_kd}
\end{align}

\subsection{Combined Training Objective}
\[
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_\text{task}
  + \alpha\,\mathcal{L}_\text{mask-KD}
  + \beta\,\mathcal{L}_\text{feat}
  + \gamma\,\mathcal{L}_\text{bnd}
\]
Optimal subset determined by ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}).

\section{Phase 5: Hyperparameter Optimisation}
\label{sec:phase5}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Optuna Multi-Fidelity Search}

25-trial Optuna study [EXP-25], 20 epochs per trial,
$\tau\in[1.5,6.0]$, $\alpha\in[0.3,3.0]$, $\beta,\gamma\in[0,1]$.
Optuna selected $\tau=1.93$, $\alpha=0.458$.

\subsection{Short-Horizon Bias Discovery}

\begin{table}[H]\centering
\caption{Temperature sensitivity at 150 epochs.}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Experiment & $\tau$ & Mask mAP$_{50}$ (150 ep.) & $\Delta$ \\
\midrule
EXP-26 (Optuna $\tau$, nb7) & 1.93 & 0.5422 & --- \\
EXP-27 (Manual $\tau$, nb5f) & \textbf{3.7769} & \textbf{0.5500} & $+$0.78 pts \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Theoretical mechanism---\emph{short-horizon bias in multi-fidelity HPO}~\cite{falkner2018bohb}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Epochs 0--20 (Optuna proxy):} Low $\tau$ produces sharp
    distributions that minimise coarse localisation loss quickly.
    Optuna rewards fast early descent.
  \item \textbf{Epochs 50--150 (full training):} Fine-grained boundary masking
    requires sustained soft gradients.
    At $\tau=1.93$, logits harden ($\sigma(z/1.93)\approx0.99$), killing
    gradients in the 6--10\,px boundary corridor after epoch~40.
    At $\tau=3.7769$, soft gradients remain active through all 150 epochs.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Locked hyperparameters:} $\tau=3.7769$, $\alpha=0.9612$, Mask-KL only.

\section{Critical Bug Discovery and Resolution}
\label{sec:bugs}
%=============================================================================

\subsubsection*{Bug 1 --- Zero-logit silent failure (nb5a--e)}

Ablation notebooks nb5a--e trained with \emph{zero} SAM~2 supervision because
the logit directory was empty; the trainer did not detect this and proceeded as
a plain fine-tune.
All ablation labels measured randomly fine-tuned models, not KD variants.
\textbf{Resolution:} mandatory assertion $N_\text{logits}>0$ before training.
nb5f rerun with assertion yields the first valid Mask-KL-only result:
Mask mAP$_{50}$ = 0.5500.

\subsubsection*{Bug 2 --- Checkpoint resolver collision (nb5e)}

\texttt{find\_checkpoint()} fell to keyword glob when explicit paths were
absent, causing the ``SegHead Frozen'' row to resolve to the DeepCrack Phase-2
checkpoint.
\textbf{Resolution:} glob fallback removed; missing checkpoints produce an
explicit warning and the run is skipped.

\subsubsection{\texttt{03\_dilated} --- Foreground-Dilated Mask-KL}
\label{sec:dilated}
Spatially adaptive weighting via 2D max-pooling dilation (kernel 9):
\[

Teaches the student the \emph{topology} of crack boundaries.
\textbf{Best in-domain Mask mAP$_{50}$ = 0.5569 (+3.1\%).}

Upsamples SAM~2 logits from $256\times256$ to $512\times512$ (bicubic).

\section{Intermediate Layer-Wise Distillation}
\label{sec:layerkd}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Motivation: Beyond Output Logits}

Output-only KD teaches the student what SAM~2 \emph{predicts}; it does not
enforce similar intermediate \emph{representations}.
For hairline cracks, the finest structural information lives in the
highest-resolution PANet feature map (P3, stride 8).
A student matching only final masks may build a brittle representational pathway
that fails under domain shift.

\subsection{The ViT-to-CNN Capacity Gap}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Raw L2 MSE forces 2.84M parameters to reproduce features from 224M
    parameters at every spatial location---an impossible optimisation target.
  \item ViT features are layer-norm normalised; CNN features are not.
    Absolute magnitudes are incompatible across architectures.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Channel-Wise Distillation (CWD)}

CWD~\cite{shu2021cwd} converts each feature channel into a spatial probability
distribution, then computes KL divergence:
\begin{align}
\hat{S}^c &= \mathrm{softmax}(S^c_\text{proj}/\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W} \\
\hat{T}^c &= \mathrm{softmax}(T^c/\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times W} \\
\mathcal{L}_\text{CWD} &= \tau^2\cdot\frac{1}{C}\sum_{c=1}^C\mathrm{KL}(\hat{T}^c\|\hat{S}^c)
\end{align}
Scale-invariant: softmax removes absolute magnitude differences.
Measures only the \emph{relative spatial activation pattern} per channel.

\subsection{Cross-Architecture Projector (CAP)}

Trainable projectors align student to teacher channel dimensions before CWD:
\[
\hat{S}_\text{proj}=\mathrm{GELU}(\mathrm{BN}(\mathrm{Conv}_{1\times1}(S)))+\mathrm{SE\text{-}Block}(S)
\]
The Squeeze-and-Excitation block recalibrates channel importance, preventing
channel collapse during distillation.

\subsection{Multi-Scale Neck Hooks (Layers 12, 15, 18)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Layer 12 (P3, stride 8):} $64\times64$ map. Richest fine-crack
    structural content. Layer weight 0.5.
  \item \textbf{Layer 15 (P4, stride 16):} $32\times32$ map. Mid-scale context.
    Layer weight 0.3.
  \item \textbf{Layer 18 (P5, stride 32):} $16\times16$ map. Coarse global
    structure. Layer weight 0.2.
\end{itemize}
Key insight: the highest structural value lives in \textbf{P3}. Distilling only
the last layer misses the representation critical for hairline cracks.
Hooks are registered only during training and discarded at inference---zero FPS
overhead at deployment.

\subsection{Offline Feature Caching}

Raw FP32 storage: $\approx$12.4\,GB. Reduced to \textbf{1.85\,GB} by FP16
quantisation and compressed NPZ archives.

\subsection{LayerKD Results and Combination Experiment}

\texttt{06\_layerkd}: OOD Mask mAP$_{50\text{-}95}$ = 0.0252 ($+28.6\%$),
tiled Dice = 0.2747 ($+13.8\%$).

\texttt{10\_hires\_layerkd\_dilated} fused CWD + foreground-dilation at
$768\times768$.
In-domain maintained (0.5426), but OOD direct mAP did not surpass
\texttt{03\_dilated} (0.0883 vs.\ 0.1007).
Finding: scaling resolution from 640 to 768 on $640\times360$ source crops

\section{OOD Evaluation and Tiled Inference}
\label{sec:ood}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{The Physical Resolution Problem}

Training crops: $640\times360$.
Uncropped survey photos: $2000\times1500$.
A 6-pixel crack becomes $6\times(512/2000)=1.5$ pixels after
downsampling---physically erased by interpolation before any neural processing.
Standard augmentation (scale, crop, flip) cannot teach detection of features
that do not survive the downsampling pipeline.

\subsection{Tiled Sliding-Window Inference Engine}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Divide $2000\times1500$ into overlapping $512\times512$ tiles
    (stride 410, 20\% overlap).
  \item Run \yolo\ on each tile at native training resolution.
  \item Stitch predictions with 2D Gaussian apodization (center-weighted
    blending, matching nnU-Net~\cite{isensee2021nnunet} and
    Cellpose~\cite{stringer2021cellpose}):
    \[w(x,y)=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(x-c_x)^2+(y-c_y)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\]
    Eliminates hard tile-boundary artifacts from flat overlap-average stitching.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Result:} All KD variants show $+52\text{--}83\%$ Dice over direct
$2000\times1500$ resize inference.

\subsection{Native-Scale Mosaic Augmentation}
\label{sec:mosaic}

\subsubsection{Root Cause: Zero Negative Tiles}
All 1,896 Crack500 training images contain crack pixels.
On raw full-resolution pavement, the model over-predicts on gravel and
oil-stain textures absent from the training distribution.

\subsubsection{Mosaic Reconstruction (\texttt{OODimprovements/01})}
Crack500 crops originated from a tiled extraction of full-resolution photos.
By detecting the crop grid structure and re-stitching adjacent crops, we
reconstructed \textbf{250 composite scenes} of $1920\times720$ pixels---genuine
full-width pavement views exceeding the $640\times360$ training ceiling.
Script: \texttt{scripts/mine\_negative\_and\_mosaic\_tiles.py}.

\subsubsection{Native-Scale Teacher Logits (\texttt{OODimprovements/02})}
SAM~2 applied to all 250 composites at native $1920\times720$.
Generated 250 native-scale logit tensors merged with 2,196 crop-scale logits
into a library of \textbf{2,446 teacher tensors}.

\subsubsection{Mosaic-Augmented Training (\texttt{OODimprovements/03})}
Retrained student on 2,156-image augmented set ($\mathrm{imgsz}=640$):
Box mAP$_{50}$=0.577, Mask mAP$_{50}$=0.544, Mask Precision=0.741 (epoch 150).
Cross-checkpoint OOD evaluation ongoing (\texttt{OODimprovements/04}).

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Ensemble Teacher Logit Fusion}

The most direct extension is to fuse logits from multiple SAM~2 prompting
strategies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Box-prompt (current):} accurate for convex blobs.
  \item \textbf{Multi-point skeleton-sampled:} $k$ points from the crack
    skeleton address non-convex elongated cracks.
  \item \textbf{Mask-refinement iterations:} uses the previous SAM~2 output as
    a memory prompt for iterative self-correction.
\end{itemize}
\[
\mathbf{Z}^t_\text{ens}=\sum_k w_k\,\mathbf{Z}^t_{(k)},\quad\sum_k w_k=1
\]
Preliminary design: box-prompt primary ($w_1=0.7$) + skeleton-sampled
multi-point ($w_2=0.3$).

\subsection{SAHI-Style Formalised Slicing}

Replace the manual tiling engine with SAHI~\cite{akyon2022sahi}-style slicing:
auto-tuned tile size and overlap, NMS-based mask merging at tile boundaries,
and multi-scale TTA (predict at 512 + 768, average predictions).

\subsection{clDice Skeleton-Aware Loss}

The clDice~\cite{shit2021cldice} loss penalises broken crack connectivity in
skeleton space:
\[
\mathcal{L}_\text{clDice}=1-\frac{2\cdot\mathrm{Prec}_\text{sk}\cdot\mathrm{Rec}_\text{sk}}
                                    {\mathrm{Prec}_\text{sk}+\mathrm{Rec}_\text{sk}}
\]
Adding this to the distillation objective would directly penalise topological
disconnection in thin crack networks.

\subsection{Mosaic-Augmented OOD Evaluation}

Running \texttt{OODimprovements/04} will measure: precision recovery on negative
background tiles; preservation of Crack500 val accuracy; whether OOD Mask
mAP$_{50}$ exceeds the 0.1007 baseline from \texttt{03\_dilated}.

\subsection{Larger Student Architectures}

YOLOv11s-seg (9.4M parameters, 21.6\,GFLOPs) at $\sim$55\,FPS.
The pipeline is architecture-agnostic; only PANet hook indices need updating.

\subsection{Publication Figure Agenda}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Side-by-side mask overlays: Baseline vs.\ \texttt{03\_dilated} vs.\
    \texttt{04\_affinity} on $2000\times1500$ asphalt samples.
  \item Temperature sensitivity curve: Mask mAP$_{50}$ vs.\ $\tau$ at epochs
    20, 50, 100, 150 (short-horizon bias crossover point).
  \item Tiled inference diagram: $2000\times1500\to$ tile grid $\to$
    Gaussian-apodized stitch.
  \item Qualitative failure modes: missed hairlines (baseline) vs.\ recovered
    hairlines (distilled) vs.\ false positives on background textures.
\end{enumerate}

%=============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%=============================================================================

CrackDistill presents a systematic knowledge-distillation framework transferring
structural priors from SAM~2 Large (224M parameters) into \yolo\ (2.84M)
for real-time road crack instance segmentation.

\noindent\textbf{Key contributions:}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Per-instance soft Mask-KL distillation with TAL-aligned target matching,
    $\tau=3.7769$, $\alpha=0.9612$.
  \item Short-horizon bias discovery: Optuna's 20-epoch proxy selects suboptimal
    $\tau$; $\tau=3.7769$ unlocks $+0.78$ mAP$_{50}$ pts at 150 epochs.
  \item Foreground-dilated spatial weighting: $+18.7\%$ OOD Mask mAP$_{50}$.
  \item Spatial pixel affinity distillation: $+3.1\%$ in-domain Mask mAP$_{50}$.
  \item Multi-scale neck CWD LayerKD on PANet layers 12, 15, 18:
    $+28.6\%$ OOD Mask mAP$_{50\text{-}95}$.
  \item Tiled inference with 2D Gaussian apodization: $+52$--$83\%$ Dice over
    direct downsampling on megapixel survey imagery.
  \item Native-scale mosaic augmentation: 250 composite pavement scenes and
    2,446 native-scale teacher logits.
  \item Cross-dataset transfer: $+5.4\%$--$+19.0\%$ Mask mAP$_{50}$.
\end{enumerate}

Edge deployment: 107.8\,FPS on Tesla T4, zero SAM~2 runtime dependency,
79$\times$ parameter compression with demonstrated OOD robustness.
